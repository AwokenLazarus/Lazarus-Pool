DATUM gateway operators: upgrade your node to Bitcoin Knots 29.4.3. —

Upgrade the Knots node your gateway uses to 29.4.3 before block 979,919; the gateway itself needs no update. 29.4.3 activates a 180-day rolling coinbase maturity: rewards from block 979,919 on unlock about 180 days after their own block, on every pool. Coins mined from block 973,440 to 979,918 unlock about 45 days after their own block. Once the fork activates, Lazarus refuses gateways whose node is on an old version when it sees one: a found block the network rejects as invalid, or work built on a chain the upgraded network rejects. A block lost to an outdated node is not compensated. Upgrading late makes the node recheck blocks at startup; pruned nodes may need a full resync.

            New to this? Start here.
            
        
            Six short answers. Mining here needs only a payout address: no account, no sign-up.
            
      
            
      
            
        
            
          1
          
            Proof of work, in one minute
            
          
            Miners race to find a hash below a target. It is pure trial and error, so more hashes win more often, and that cost is what makes the chain hard to rewrite.
            
          How the pool fits in →
        
            
        
            
          2
          
            Why this chain uses BLAKE2b
            
          
            At block 961,640 (30 August 2026) this chain switched from SHA-256d to BLAKE2b. Supply, halvings, addresses and history are unchanged, and Siacoin ASICs now mine it.
            
          The fork, and BIP-110 →
        
            
        
            
          3
          
            What DATUM changes
            
          
            On an ordinary pool, the pool picks what goes in the block. With your own DATUM gateway and Knots node, you build the template and the pool only splits the reward. Here that costs 0%.
            
          Set up a DATUM gateway →
        
            
        
            
          4
          
            How TIDES pays
            
          
            TIDES pays on a rolling window of about eight network-difficulties of work. When the pool finds a block, every address with work in the window is paid its share. No rounds, no resets.
            
          TIDES explained →
        
            
        
            
          5
          
            Paid in the block itself
            
          
            Each payout is an output of the block’s own coinbase, sent straight to your address. No balance, no threshold, no withdrawal, and every payout is on chain.
            
          Non-custodial payouts →
        
            
        
            
          6
          
            A pool that caps itself
            
          
            The public stratum is capped at 15% of network hashrate; past that, new connections are relayed to other pools. Your own DATUM gateway never counts toward the cap.
            
          The 15% self-cap →
        
            
      
            
      
            
        
            
          
            From zero to mining, the DATUM way
            
          
              
            
            1. Get a payout address. Any wallet that gives you a bc1… address on this chain. That address is your username; there is nothing to register.
              2. 
            
            2. Run your own node. Bitcoin Knots for this chain, with server=1 and a cookie or RPC user your gateway can read. Your node is what chooses the transactions in your blocks.
              3. 
            
            3. Start a DATUM gateway. Build one (CONVOY is recommended), paste in the Lazarus host, port and pubkey from the Connect page, and set mining.pool_address to your address.
              4. 
            
            4. Point your miner at your gateway. Any Siacoin BLAKE2b ASIC, aimed at your own gateway’s stratum port, with the username address.worker.
              5. 
            
            5. Watch it work. Your gateway shows up under Gateways and your address under Miners. You pay 0%, and you build your own blocks.

            Words you will meet
            
          
            
          
            Hashrate
            Hashes tried per second. 1 TH/s is a trillion a second; 1 PH/s is a thousand TH/s.
            
          
            Share
            A hash good enough to prove you are working, though not good enough to be a block. Shares are how the pool measures your work.
            
          
            Difficulty
            How hard the network’s target is. It retargets so blocks keep their pace as hashrate comes and goes.
            
          
            Coinbase
            The first transaction in a block, which creates the reward. Here it pays miners directly.
            
          
            Block template
            The draft block a miner hashes: which transactions are in it, and who the coinbase pays.
            
          
            Window
            The stretch of recent shares TIDES pays on. Here it is eight network-difficulties of work.
            
          
            Gateway
            Your own DATUM software. It talks to your node for templates and to the pool for the reward split.
            
          
            Orphan
            A valid block that lost the race to another found at the same moment. It pays nothing.
            
          
            
        
            
      
            
    
            
  
            

  
            
    
            
      
            
        
            How it works
            
        
            How your share becomes a payout.
            
        
            For newcomers and operators alike. Your ASIC never sees any of this — it hashes a plain BLAKE2b header.
            
      
            
      
              
        
            1. 
          01
          
              You hash, the pool counts
              
          
              Point your machines at your own DATUM gateway. Prime rebuilds and checks every share, and credits accepted work to your address in the TIDES window.
              
        
              2. 
        
            2. 
          02
          
              The window decides the split
              
          
              The window holds the last 8 × network difficulty of accepted work — days of pool work at typical hashrate, not the last hour. A found block pays each address in proportion to its share of that window, less its fee. A new rig starts near 0% and ramps as its work enters and older work ages out.
              
        
              3. 
        
            3. 
          03
          
              The block pays you directly
              
          
              Prime hands every gateway the same coinbase output list — the one shown under Next payout. A share whose coinbase pays anything else is refused, so whoever finds the block, it pays the window. There is no pool balance to withdraw and no operator to trust with your coins.
