1When you buy

  1. You find a shop in Browse. Its listing is signed and published on Nostr: what it sells, the prices, and how to reach it.
  2. You allow the shop in your spending rules, which you sign with your own key. The signer in your app pays nothing outside them.
  3. You open the shop's service: a seller's whole web app (for example Jellyfin) in a sealed visit, your local AI address, or a file. Your browser or AI app talks only to your own Stream app, on your own network.
  4. Your app connects to the seller's app. Today that is over the seller's https address; with the iroh link (in progress) it is by the seller's key, end to end encrypted, with no address needed, and your app first checks that the seller's Lightning node vouches for that key.
  5. The seller answers with its price. Your app pays a small ticket from your own node, sends the request, and the seller meters what it delivers to the millisatoshi and signs a receipt. The next ticket is paid just before the last runs out.

2When you sell

  1. You point your Stream app at a service on your own network, set a price, and press Sell. Your app signs a listing and publishes it on Nostr.
  2. Your gateway sits in front of the service: it states the price, meters every answer, signs receipts, and passes the request on. With buyer accounts (in progress) each buyer gets their own account in your app under a private handle; with busy limits (in progress) new buyers are turned away before they pay when your box is full.
  3. You are paid into your own node. Nobody holds the money in between.
  4. With the iroh link (in progress) you need no domain, certificate, tunnel or port forward: buyers reach you by your key, directly or through a relay.

3When you rent hashrate

  1. A hashmaker lists its miners; a hashtaker rents them into its own DATUM Gateway, which builds the block template and checks every share.
  2. The hashmaker's app carries the miners' work to the hashtaker's app. The link between them is encrypted end to end. Over iroh (in progress) neither side needs a port forward.
  3. The hashtaker pays for each slice of work after it is delivered, counting only the shares its own gateway accepted.
  4. Rented rigs from rental sites reaching a hashtaker's DATUM Gateway without a port forward is designed, not yet built.

4How the money moves

Every service pays the same way: small tickets of about 900 sat over XBT Lightning, from the buyer's own node to the seller's own node, drawn down exactly as the service is used and refilled just before they run out. A buyer can lose at most one ticket to a seller that stops serving. Relays and the iroh link never carry money, and Stream never holds anyone's funds. Paying from an NWC wallet instead of your own node is designed, not yet built.

5What the labels mean

  • No label: released, in the app today.
  • In progress: built and in testing, not yet released: the iroh link and relays built into Stream, sellers' whole web apps with buyer accounts, busy limits, VPN exits, and relays paid per GB.
  • Designed, not yet built: planned, with a design, not in the app: local links for TVs and native apps, paying from an NWC wallet, Nostr DVMs as AI sellers, a Blossom media server sold by the MiB, and rented rigs reaching a DATUM Gateway with no port forward.

The changelog lists what each release added, and progress and status what is built, planned and not built.
